While concerns about political interference in sports are valid, the decision to strip Senegal of their African Champions title and award it to Morocco should be viewed through the lens of regulatory compliance and fairness, not merely politics. Sports governing bodies operate based on established rules and procedures to maintain integrity. If Senegal violated any regulations—whether related to player eligibility, doping, or administrative issues—the enforcement of these rules upholds the principle of fair play rather than undermines it. Ignoring such infractions for fear of political backlash would erode trust in the governing bodies’ capacity to enforce standards impartially.

Moreover, this action can serve as a precedent to improve transparency and accountability in African football, rather than diminish it. In many global sports contexts, including UEFA or FIFA, titles have been revoked when rules were breached, regardless of the teams’ popularity or political weight. The ultimate goal is to ensure that victories are earned within the agreed framework, preserving the sport’s integrity over time.

Accusing the decision of being politically motivated without evidence risks perpetuating victimhood narratives and distracts from systemic improvements needed in sports governance. Instead, the focus should be on strengthening institutions, clarifying regulations, and implementing unbiased adjudication mechanisms so that all teams, including Senegal and Morocco, compete on a level playing field. This approach will build genuine confidence among fans and players, reinforcing rather than harming African football’s credibility globally.
